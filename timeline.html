SOURCE-CONTROLLED CHRONOLOGY · V9

A timestamp means little until we know what it timestamps.

This chronology separates the time an event allegedly occurred, the time someone reported it, the time a system or document recorded it, and the time a witness later described it. Conflicting entries remain visible until an authenticated source resolves them.

Event timeReport timeRecord timeLater testimony

TIME-TYPE KEY

Four different clocks appear in this case.

A later-created report can describe an earlier event. A system timestamp can reflect data entry rather than occurrence. Testimony years later can clarify or conflict with contemporaneous records.

Event time

When something occurred

Examples include responder arrival, patient contact, pronouncement, document signing, or cremation.

Report time

When information was communicated

Examples include the 911 call, OCME notification, a statement to police, or a request sent to a custodian.

Record time

When a system or document captured it

Examples include CAD entries, EDRS events, filed orders, email transmission, or docket entries.

Later account

When a person later recalled it

Deposition testimony and family witness accounts must remain attributed and separated from contemporaneous records.

REPORT-TIME CONFLICT

Accounts of the critical window differ.

Police, EMS, OCME, and attributed family accounts use different descriptions of departure purpose, location, phone contact, and return time.

What the comparison establishes

  • Police recorded a work account.
  • EMS recorded fishing.
  • The family reports a father-and-son fishing account.
  • OCME later used charter-boat wording.

What remains unresolved

  • Whether the wording reflects distinct accounts, paraphrase, or overlapping activity.
  • Exact phone, location, travel, office, marina, purchase, and return-time facts.
  • Whether any discrepancy was intentional.
Discriminating evidence: native phone and location records, office access and alarm data, marina or charter records, vehicle and toll data, purchases, surveillance, and separately collected witness statements.
