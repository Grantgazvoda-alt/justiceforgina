# Justice for Gina V8 — Branch and Version Benchmark

Prepared July 29, 2026.

## Purpose

This benchmark compares the recoverable Justice for Gina site versions and release branches against the current Mom project requirements. No earlier version is adopted wholesale. Strong components are preserved, factual or privacy regressions are rejected, and the final V8 is rebuilt from the best verified parts.

## Evaluation criteria

1. Evidence coverage and source traceability.
2. Separation of findings, records, testimony, opinions, family beliefs, and unresolved issues.
3. Privacy and publication control.
4. Public usability and visual clarity.
5. Local availability of approved public material.
6. Structured data and revision history.
7. Fundraising usefulness and accountability.
8. Accessibility, mobile behavior, security, and deployment resilience.
9. Consistency with the latest Project Memory, chronology, proof matrix, completion audit, and missing-record queue.

## Version 3 — Multi-page public platform

### Strengths

- Established the multi-page architecture.
- Added evidence, timeline, press, support, standards, sitemap, manifest, mobile navigation, reduced-motion handling, and a responsible fallback page.
- Created a solemn and usable base design.

### Weaknesses

- Predates major 2026 probate, OCME, deposition, financial, and external-dependency findings.
- Has little structured evidence provenance.
- Uses broad narrative pages that no longer reflect the controlled project record.

### Decision

Preserve the general information architecture and accessibility foundations. Do not preserve V3 content status or branding as the current release.

## Version 4 — Evidence intelligence

### Strengths

- Added a canonical public evidence dataset.
- Added provenance, verification, sensitivity, publication authority, relationships, page-citation fields, and revision history.
- Added a record viewer and source-supersession policy.
- Correctly kept restricted medical and sensitive source files outside the public repository.

### Weaknesses

- Dataset and pages are stale and incomplete.
- Many records have no page-level citations.
- The Police and EMS record conflicts with the approved redacted public release status shown elsewhere.
- Major controlled findings and evidence groups are absent.
- The record viewer remains branded and described as V4.

### Decision

Retain the schema, record-viewer concept, provenance model, sensitivity controls, and revision-history model. Rebuild the dataset as V8 and reconcile every visible page against it.

## Version 5 — Funding transparency

### Strengths

- Separates lawful court, records, expert, preservation, and communication work.
- States that donors do not control conclusions and no outcome is guaranteed.
- Includes tax, privacy, privilege, and donor-control safeguards.

### Weaknesses

- Uses broad categories without a maintained budget framework or update cadence.
- Does not provide a donor progress system.
- Conversion paths are inconsistent across the site.
- Some navigation and release metadata remain V4/V5.

### Decision

Retain donor safeguards and broad lawful-use categories. Expand them into a maintainable court-and-records accountability system without inventing amounts or exposing privilege.

## Version 6 — Family position and local public document library

### Strengths

- Strongest recoverable local public-document architecture.
- Uses stable first-party routes instead of depending entirely on Google Drive embeds.
- Contains privacy-protected summaries for probate decrees, dispatch records, Police and EMS comparisons, packet inventories, expert reports, and working-document status.
- Clearly identifies document type, source status, privacy limits, and what a record cannot establish.
- Separates documented material, family position, and unresolved questions in the interface.

### Weaknesses

- Homepage and family-position copy make prominent allegations against a named person and are not appropriate for the stricter current public standard.
- Some summaries predate later source corrections and project-memory findings.
- Several pages use V6 staging labels, outdated routes, and incomplete source citations.
- The local library does not yet contain the newer OCME, PC-242, Panwar/828, Kate Forte, funeral-home, Stone, Wells Fargo, and external-dependency material.

### Decision

Port and improve the local public-document architecture and privacy-safe summary format. Do not import the accusatory family-theory presentation. Rewrite every imported page under the V8 source-classification standard and current Project Memory.

## Partial Version 8 — Public command center

### Strengths

- Strongest visual design and public hierarchy.
- Strongest “what the record establishes / what it does not establish” framework.
- Separates probate findings, pending charges, OCME records, sworn testimony, and unresolved evidence.
- Provides a clear command-center homepage, case-status page, improved fallback, and security-header update.

### Weaknesses

- Only the homepage and case-status page are truly authored as V8.
- Most public pages remain V4/V5 in source and are cosmetically relabeled at runtime.
- Structured data remains V4.
- Major new evidence and completed work are absent.
- No complete local document library, public-claim register, domain-recovery architecture, or full release QA exists.

### Decision

Use the V8 design system, command-center navigation, classification badges, proof-and-limit panels, unresolved-evidence layout, and public safeguards as the final presentation layer. Complete—not replace—the architecture.

## Selected composite architecture

The completed V8 will use:

- **V8** for visual design, navigation, command-center hierarchy, case-status classification, proof/limit presentation, and release identity.
- **V6** for local public-document routes, privacy-protected source summaries, and document-library usability.
- **V4** for structured record metadata, provenance, sensitivity, authority, relationships, citations, and revision history after full reconciliation.
- **V5** for donor safeguards and lawful-use categories, expanded into a maintainable accountability model.
- **Current Project Memory and controlled work products** as the only authority for updated factual content and wording.

## Material that will not be carried forward

- Prominent statements accusing a named person of causing Gina's death.
- Claims that a discrepancy, missing record, rapid process, family belief, or apparent procedural conflict proves a crime.
- Stale V3–V6 release labels or obsolete navigation.
- Direct publication of restricted medical, private, privileged, sealed, unredacted, or source-device material.
- Reliance on JavaScript to repair core navigation, release identity, or legal wording.
- Contradictory public and structured-data statuses.

## First reconstruction slice

1. Restore a V8 local public-document library.
2. Port and update the probate-decree, dispatch-timeline, and Police/EMS comparison pages.
3. Add V8 records for those materials and correct the approved Police/EMS release status.
4. Expand case status with PC-242, timing-conflict, Kate Forte, and external-dependency modules.
5. Rebuild the evidence archive and timeline against the reconciled V8 dataset.

## Release rule

No completion branch is merged merely because it looks better. It must also be factually current, source-classified, privacy-safe, internally consistent, accessible, deployable, and externally verifiable.
