WATERBURY PROBATE COURT · APRIL 22, 2026

The court invalidated the July 28, 2019 will based on fraud.

Two signed decrees concern the Estate of Gina Marie Gazvoda. They are court findings about a will contest and estate administration—not findings about cause of death or criminal responsibility for Gina’s death.

Court-generatedSigned decreesScope preservedPrivacy protected
COURT FINDING

Will contest

The operative language concerns the original will dated July 28, 2019 that had been admitted to probate.

What the decree establishes

  • Grant E. Gazvoda and Garrison Gazvoda challenged the will.
  • The parties stipulated that the will was fraudulent.
  • The court revoked and invalidated the will based on fraud pursuant to Connecticut General Statutes § 45a-251.

What it does not establish

  • Who caused Gina’s death.
  • Who authored or altered every disputed document.
  • Criminal guilt.
  • The validity of every transaction or designation outside the will addressed by the decree.
COURT ORDER

Estate administration

A second decree changed who was authorized to administer the estate and ordered additional filings.

Orders entered

  • Nicholas Forte was removed as Executor.
  • Attorney Edward Duffy was appointed Administrator.
  • A final financial report was ordered by May 31, 2026.
  • The successor administrator was ordered to file an inventory within the period stated in the decree.

Separate questions

  • The decree does not itself decide whether estate funds paid civil-defense fees.
  • It does not itself classify the later PC-242 distributions as theft, misuse, or authorized expense.
  • Financial tracing requires the accounting, settlement, bank, invoice, trust-ledger, and authorization records.
