LABEL MAP

Each description must be tied to its source.

A relationship term can be social, demographic, administrative, or legal. The source, author, date, and purpose determine its evidentiary weight.

OCME INTAKE

Boyfriend / “Other”

The OCME contact record identifies Nicholas as boyfriend and classifies the relationship as “Other.” This is an intake description, not a legal determination of custody or next-of-kin status.

OTHER RECORDS

Significant other / partner

Other controlled records use significant-other or partner language. These terms describe a relationship but do not independently establish statutory custody and control.

Nicholas signed representations that he was next of kin, had custody and control, possessed full legal authority, and knew of no objection. Those are documented representations; their legal basis remains disputed.

VERIFIED DISCREPANCY

What the comparison establishes

The descriptions are not uniform across the record and should not be collapsed into a single uncontested status.

Supported

  • Multiple relationship and authority labels appear in controlled records.
  • A signed probate petition states surviving spouse: none.
  • A signed cremation authorization contains next-of-kin and authority representations.

Not established

  • Who supplied every label.
  • Whether a funeral-home field was legally operative.
  • Whether the claimed custody and control was valid under § 45a-318.
  • Whether anyone knowingly made a false representation.

DECISIVE RECORDS

Authority requires provenance, not shorthand.

The highest-value records are the authenticated designation or directive, original funeral-home intake and source-attribution records, the § 20-230c form, employee notes, and testimony identifying who supplied each field.

Evidence limit: Inconsistent labels justify further records work. They do not, by themselves, prove fraud, unlawful cremation, or criminal intent.
