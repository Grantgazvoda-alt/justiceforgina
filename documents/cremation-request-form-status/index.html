CONNECTICUT CREMATION DOCUMENTATION

Connecticut law requires a signed cremation-request form and at least six years of retention.

The completed original form has not been located in the controlled funeral-home production. That absence raises a defined records question; it does not yet establish whether the form was withheld, lost, destroyed, or never completed.

STATUTORY REQUIREMENT

What § 20-230c requires

When cremation is requested by the person with custody and control under § 45a-318, the funeral director must complete a written form containing specified information.

Required contents and signatures

  • Funeral service business name and address.
  • Deceased person's name.
  • Place and time of cremation.
  • Licensed funeral director or embalmer.
  • Name and address of the person claiming custody and control.
  • Requested disposition of the cremated remains.
  • Signatures and dates of the person claiming custody and control and the funeral director.

Retention and limits

  • The original signed form must be maintained by the funeral service business for at least six years.
  • The statute identifies a required record; it does not by itself decide whether a particular cremation was unlawful.
  • The legal effect of any designation or claimed authority requires the underlying documents and counsel review.

CONTROLLED PRODUCTION STATUS

The form should answer who claimed authority.

The statutory form is materially different from a generic cremation authorization because it should identify the person claiming custody and control, the funeral director, the requested disposition, and the signature dates.

Four possible explanations remain open: the form exists outside the produced set; it existed but was lost or destroyed; a different record was treated as the statutory form; or the form was never completed. The present absence does not distinguish among them.

TARGETED REQUEST

Request the record by statute, system, and version.

A complete request should seek the original signed form, every copy and version, source and intake records, authority documents, scanner metadata, retention history, and a custodian certification identifying all systems searched.

Official statutory sources: Connecticut General Statutes § 20-230c and Connecticut General Statutes § 45a-318.

RELATED REVIEW

The form must be read alongside the authority documents and relationship labels.

No one label—partner, spouse, next of kin, legal custodian, or agent—should be treated as legally operative without its source and governing authority.
