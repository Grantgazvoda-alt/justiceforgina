MISSING RECORDS · CUSTODIANS · DISCRIMINATING EVIDENCE

The unresolved questions now depend mainly on authenticated records held by outside custodians.

This map identifies what evidence could confirm, narrow, contradict, or disprove current propositions. It does not assume that every listed record exists or that a custodian's nonproduction proves concealment.

TOP-TIER DEPENDENCIES

Records capable of materially resolving central conflicts.

These categories have the highest combined value for timing, provenance, authority, medical causation, financial tracing, and independent corroboration.

VITAL RECORDS / EDRS

Death-certificate version history

First and corrected forms, field changes, user identities, timestamps, rejection and correction events, certifier support, and audit history.

OCME

Complete inquiry and cremation approval

Full intake, calls, notes, review, approval, designee identity, timestamp, and any reason or policy basis for declination.

FUNERAL HOME / CREMATORY

Authority, intake, timing, and native communications

Original § 20-230c form, designation records, source attribution, emails, call logs, scanner records, crematory timing, retention history, and completeness certification.

WELLS FARGO

Bank-held death-certificate image

Exact image, every version, submission source, scan and upload metadata, workflow identifiers, notes, access history, and retention evidence.

ORIGINAL DOCUMENT CUSTODIANS

Directive and designation originals

Physical originals, earliest-generation scans, production histories, native email attachments, custodian declarations, and source-device metadata.

DIGITAL TIMELINE SOURCES

Critical-window activity

Phone and location records, office access and alarm data, marina or charter records, vehicle and toll data, purchases, surveillance, and source-device logs.

MEDICAL AND FORENSIC

Cause-of-death questions require competent source material.

The absence of autopsy and toxicology limits retrospective certainty. Medical records can test natural-disease support but cannot recreate every lost postmortem examination opportunity.

Needed medical sources

  • Complete treating-provider records and communications.
  • Contemporaneous certifier rationale and source records.
  • Medication, infusion, home-care, nursing, and pharmacy records.
  • Native photographs and media with documented custody.

Expert questions

  • Whether the certified causal chain is medically supported.
  • What competing natural, traumatic, toxicologic, or other explanations remain.
  • What can and cannot be inferred after cremation.
  • Which conclusions require a forensic pathologist, toxicologist, or other specialist.
FINANCIAL AND LITIGATION

Transactions and productions require complete tracing.

An account entry, exact-dollar match, or absent family copy does not establish the underlying source, authorization, purpose, or legal treatment.

Financial proof targets

  • Settlement statements and trust ledgers.
  • Estate bank statements and canceled checks.
  • Retainers, invoices, matter numbers, time entries, and receipts.
  • Payment-source, recipient, reimbursement, and authorization records.

Litigation proof targets

  • Authenticated discovery answers and objections.
  • Privilege logs and production indexes.
  • Native productions, cover letters, load files, and Bates histories.
  • Compliance orders, extensions, motions, rulings, and counsel certifications.

STATUS CONTROL

Every dependency receives a precise disposition.

Use: verified complete, controlled negative search, requested, pending, partially produced, denied with stated basis, unavailable, counsel-controlled, witness path, expert-required, or approval-gated.

Evidence limit: The family archive's lack of a record does not prove that the record never existed, that a custodian still has it, or that anyone intentionally withheld or destroyed it.
