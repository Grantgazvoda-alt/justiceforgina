CONTROLLED TIMING FINDING

What the current timing evidence supports

The available recorded dates and times place the cremation beyond the 48-hour interval from the recorded death.

Supported

  • The controlled record contains a recorded death time and a later cremation time.
  • The interval appears to satisfy the applicable 48-hour waiting period.
  • The timing issue is distinct from custody, consent, statutory-form, and OCME-approval questions.

Not established

  • That every crematory timestamp is native, complete, or independently certified.
  • Who entered or transmitted each time field.
  • That authority and documentation requirements were satisfied merely because the waiting period was met.
  • That cremation was illegal.

NATIVE RECORDS NEEDED

Obtain the crematory's complete operational and custody trail.

The decisive records include intake and release times, authorization receipt, OCME approval receipt, operator identity, retort or machine log, internal case number, custody transfers, permit records, and custodian certification.

INTAKE

Receipt and custody

Date and time received, source, transporter, identification process, refrigeration, and custody-transfer records.

AUTHORIZATION

Approval package received

Which forms and permits arrived, when they arrived, who reviewed them, and whether any deficiency was corrected.

OPERATION

Cremation log

Operator, machine or retort identifier, start and completion times, case number, and contemporaneous electronic or paper log.

CERTIFICATION

Custodian statement

Systems searched, record-retention policy, completeness, corrections, unavailable records, and any migration or deletion history.

Evidence limit: A compliant waiting interval does not resolve whether the correct person had authority or whether all required forms and approvals were complete.

RELATED QUESTIONS

Focus the remaining review on authority and documentation.

The statutory form, custody-and-control basis, source attribution, and full approval trail are more discriminating than repeating an unsupported waiting-period allegation.
