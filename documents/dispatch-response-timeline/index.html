UNRESOLVED RECORD CONFLICT

The 17:15 narrative entry

A police narrative includes a 17:15 dispatch/pronouncement entry that does not align with the surrounding dispatch, EMS, and OCME times.

What can be stated

  • The 17:15 entry appears in the controlled police narrative.
  • Other controlled records cluster around approximately 19:06–19:15 for dispatch, contact, and pronouncement.
  • The entries are materially inconsistent and require custodian clarification.

What cannot yet be stated

  • That the 17:15 entry was intentionally fabricated.
  • That it was definitively a typographical error.
  • Who entered or approved it.
  • Whether an agency correction, audit trail, or supplemental report exists.
Resolution target: Obtain the native CAD export, report audit history, officer notes, supervisor review, correction logs, and custodian explanation. Do not silently replace 17:15 with 19:15.

EVIDENCE LIMITS

Response timestamps do not determine cause of death.

The sequence organizes what agencies recorded. It does not establish what occurred before the call, when irreversible death occurred, whether every statement was accurate, or whether a crime occurred.

OCME distinction: The intake narrative included attributed statements such as “nothing suspicious” and “no trauma.” The controlled file does not document an independent OCME body examination, so those statements must not be presented as OCME examination findings.
