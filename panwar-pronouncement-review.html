DEATH CERTIFICATE · STATUTORY CONFLICT

The pronouncer entry appears illegal on the present record.

Dr. Nirmala Panwar testified under oath that she did not see Gina Gazvoda's body. The death certificate nevertheless identifies her as the practitioner who pronounced Gina dead and also as the medical certifier.

WHAT IS VERIFIED

The record-to-testimony conflict

Sworn testimony

No body viewing

Panwar repeatedly testified that she did not see or examine Gina's body before signing the certificate.

Official record

Named as pronouncer

The death certificate identifies Panwar in the pronouncement fields and again in the medical-certification fields.

Missing audit trail

No identified alternate pronouncer

The face of the certificate does not identify the EMS practitioner who apparently made the actual determination and pronouncement.

Current public finding: Because the named pronouncer testified that she never viewed the body, the pronouncement entry appears to violate Connecticut General Statutes § 7-62b(c). This is an apparent statutory violation, not a final adjudication of civil, criminal, licensing, or disciplinary liability.

THE 2021 LAW

Personal viewing was required unless the signer was only a different medical certifier

Connecticut General Statutes § 7-62b(c), as in effect in 2021, prohibited a physician or other listed professional from signing and returning the medical certification unless that professional personally viewed and examined the body and was satisfied the person was dead. The statute excused a second viewing only when the medical certifier was different from the person who made the determination and pronouncement of death.

CONTROLLING LAW2021 statute

Connecticut General Statutes § 7-62b(c)

The exception applies to a later medical certifier who did not make the pronouncement. It does not support identifying a non-viewing physician as the pronouncer.
