ACCOUNTABILITY MODEL

Every material expense should answer five questions.

Good accounting supports donor trust without exposing privileged strategy or private evidence.

What was purchased?

A record, filing, professional service, preservation tool, accessibility service, or communications deliverable.

Why was it necessary?

The evidentiary, legal, preservation, privacy, accessibility, or public-information need it addressed.

Who approved it?

The family or authorized decision-maker responsible for the expense.

What supports it?

An invoice, receipt, engagement record, filing confirmation, contract, or other reliable documentation.

What can be disclosed?

A public summary when lawful, while protecting privilege, strategy, medical data, witness information, and account details.

Transparency boundary: Responsible accounting does not require publishing privileged legal strategy, witness identities, private medical records, personal addresses, account numbers, payment credentials, or source-device data.

DONOR FAQ

Questions worth asking before contributing.

Does a contribution guarantee court, agency, or expert action?

No. Courts, agencies, custodians, experts, and counsel make independent decisions. Support funds lawful work; it cannot guarantee an outcome.

Is a contribution tax-deductible?

This website does not represent that contributions are tax-deductible. Review the fundraiser's disclosures and consult a qualified tax professional.

Can donors direct the investigation or demand publication?

No. Donor preferences do not control evidence classifications, legal strategy, witness contact, expert conclusions, or publication decisions.

Will all spending be published?

Public summaries may be provided when lawful and useful. Privilege, strategy, private evidence, personal data, and security information remain protected.

How can a professional help without donating?

Qualified professionals may contact the project through the press and professional-review page.
