PLANNED USES

Five workstreams support one objective: a reliable record.

Actual spending depends on legal needs, availability of records and experts, and the family’s priorities. Categories are stated broadly to protect strategy and private information.

01

Court and counsel costs

Filing expenses, transcript acquisition, certified copies, litigation support, and qualified legal review connected to the family’s lawful claims and defenses.

02

Records and authentication

Public-record fees, certified vital and probate records, native electronic files, metadata preservation, document comparison, and chain-of-custody work.

03

Qualified expert review

Independent review by appropriately credentialed professionals where expertise is needed, with scope, limitations, and source materials documented.

04

Secure evidence handling

Protected storage, backups, hashing, controlled working copies, privacy review, redaction, and preservation of original files and communications.

05

Public-interest communication

Website maintenance, accessible source summaries, reporter materials, lawful advocacy, and careful updates that distinguish findings from allegations.

ACCOUNTABILITY MODEL

Every material expense should answer four questions.

What was purchased?

A record, professional service, filing, preservation tool, or communications deliverable.

Why was it necessary?

The specific evidentiary, legal, preservation, or public-information need it addressed.

What supports the expense?

An invoice, receipt, engagement record, filing confirmation, or other reliable documentation.

What can be disclosed?

A public summary when lawful, while protecting privilege, litigation strategy, medical information, and personal data.

Transparency boundary: Responsible accounting does not require publishing privileged legal strategy, witness information, private medical records, personal addresses, account numbers, or other sensitive data.

DONOR FAQ

Questions worth asking before contributing.

Does a contribution guarantee that a court or agency will act?

No. Courts, law-enforcement agencies, regulators, experts, and record custodians make independent decisions. Support funds lawful work; it cannot guarantee an outcome.

Is a contribution tax-deductible?

This website does not represent that contributions are tax-deductible. Review the fundraiser’s current disclosures and consult a qualified tax professional for advice specific to you.

Will donor names or amounts be published?

Not by this website without permission or a lawful reason. The fundraising platform may apply its own visibility and privacy settings.

Can donors direct the investigation or demand publication?

No. Donor preferences do not control evidence classifications, legal strategy, witness contact, or publication decisions.

How can a professional help without donating?

Qualified attorneys, investigators, journalists, victim advocates, archivists, accessibility specialists, and relevant experts may contact the project through the press and professional-review page.
